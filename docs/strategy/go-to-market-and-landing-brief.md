# Go-to-Market and Landing Brief

Status: `Proposed` — follows the `VALIDATE FIRST` decision  
Last reviewed: 2026-07-30  
Primary market: Germany  
Pilot geography: Berlin/Brandenburg only if owner-confirmed

## Objective

Validate whether independent restaurants and cafés will pay for a managed illuminated-valance
retrofit at a sustainable margin, and whether LICHTSAUM can deliver it safely and repeatably.

The first 90 days are not a national launch. They are a controlled local product test with:

- one narrow product;
- one primary segment;
- one compatibility-led CTA;
- itemised manual quotes;
- a physical sample and paid projects as proof;
- partner and direct outreach before search scale.

The governing decision is in [Product–Market Decision](product-market-decision.md). Market numbers,
competitors, source dates and caveats are in
[Market and Competitor Evidence](market-and-competitor-evidence.md).

## GTM hypothesis

### Ideal customer

An independent restaurant or café:

- operates after dusk or values evening frontage;
- has an existing, street-facing commercial awning with a separate valance;
- is opening, rebranding, refurbishing or replacing worn textile;
- has a visible wordmark/logo that can work within verified letter-height constraints;
- can identify the venue owner/tenant and the façade decision path;
- is prepared to buy a designed and installed project rather than the cheapest replacement fabric;
- lies within the confirmed local service radius.

### Buying roles

| Role | Job / concern | Required answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner/operator | “Will this strengthen the frontage without replacing my awning?” | Clear before/after concept, total scope, price and timing |
| Brand/designer | “Will the logo look controlled by day and night?” | Artwork limits, colour/fabric choices, design proof |
| Landlord/property owner | “Is the façade and tenancy responsibility clear?” | Drawings, attachment, appearance and permission boundary |
| Awning installer | “Will it fit and move safely?” | Geometry, Keder, weight, cable route and manufacturer limits |
| Electrician | “What exactly is supplied and connected?” | Diagram, driver/enclosure, ratings and handover scope |
| Authority/heritage contact | “What is changing and how is it illuminated?” | Object-specific drawing, photomontage and technical description |

One person may fill several roles in an independent venue. The lead flow must identify missing roles
before production.

### Jobs to be done

Primary:

> When I am opening, rebranding or refreshing a venue that already has an awning, help me make the
> brand readable and controlled after dark without buying a complete new awning, while making the
> compatibility, field work and local-rule responsibilities clear before I commit.

Supporting:

- replace a worn or outdated valance with current branding;
- obtain one coherent design/production/installation path;
- understand whether the existing awning, cable route and façade are suitable;
- compare illumination with unlit print, a separate sign and a whole new awning.

The job is not “buy an LED panel.” It is to reduce uncertainty around a small but cross-disciplinary
façade project.

### Likely objections

| Objection | Required response |
| --- | --- |
| “Why not just print a new valance?” | Show the day/night difference honestly; do not invent footfall uplift |
| “Will it fit my awning?” | Photo/measurement pre-check and explicit incompatibility rules |
| “What does the complete project cost?” | Itemised range/quote; distinguish product, field work, electrical and special conditions |
| “Do I need permission?” | Object-specific review; never universal `genehmigungsfrei` |
| “Who connects and warrants it?” | Named contractor and written responsibility |
| “How does it handle rain and movement?” | Verified complete-system documents; no unsupported `wasserdicht` claim |
| “How long will it take?” | Only an owner-approved and supplier-backed schedule |
| “Can I see a real result?” | Physical sample plus rights-cleared day/night project |
| “What happens if the panel fails?” | Written service, access, spare and replacement path |

## Offer architecture

### 1. Flagship: managed one-zone retrofit

Proposed internal standard:

- existing compatible commercial awning;
- one 2400 × 200 mm illuminated field;
- 300 mm finished valance;
- logo or restrained wordmark;
- photo pre-check, measurement where needed and design proof;
- fabricated product and delivery;
- optional local removal, installation and electrical connection, only where operationally
  confirmed;
- authority-document support as a separately scoped service, never approval assurance.

Public category:

`Beleuchteter Markisen-Volant für bestehende Gewerbemarkisen`

Primary CTA:

`Projekt prüfen lassen`

### 2. Supporting custom project

Use 1200 × 150, 1200 × 300 or 1–3 illuminated fields when the verified geometry and artwork require
them. Do not present panel dimensions as lifestyle “models.” They are engineering choices inside an
outcome-led offer.

### 3. Partner supply pilot

For selected awning/sign partners:

- compatible-valance specification;
- artwork/BAT workflow;
- fabricated valance and technical pack;
- explicit allocation of measurement, installation, electrical work and warranty;
- referral or resale economics.

Do not publish the partner programme before supplier, liability, territory and margin terms are
signed.

### 4. Deferred multi-site rollout

Chains, franchise systems and architects may become valuable after:

- a standard one-site delivery pack exists;
- repeat geometry/CI handling is proven;
- service and replacement coverage exists;
- project evidence meets procurement expectations.

No national rollout claim belongs in the first release.

## Message system

### Core

| Layer | Proposed German wording | Use / evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category | `Beleuchteter Markisen-Volant für bestehende Gewerbemarkisen` | Precise working category; owner must confirm capability |
| Main promise | `Aus Ihrer bestehenden Markise wird Markenlicht.` | Conceptual line; immediately explain the physical product |
| Supporting explanation | `Ein individuell gestalteter Leuchtvolant ersetzt den vorhandenen Volant – nach Prüfung von Markise, Einbausituation und Stromweg.` | Does not imply universal fit |
| Primary CTA | `Projekt prüfen lassen` | Neutral; no `kostenlos` or `unverbindlich` until approved |
| Secondary CTA | `So funktioniert der Retrofit` | Anchor to process, not a competing lead action |

`Markenlicht` is a positioning phrase, not a claim that the product is a legally recognised sign
category or produces measured business results.

### Reasons to believe

Only publish a reason after its evidence exists:

1. real day/night sample or project;
2. clear compatibility checklist and rejection cases;
3. exact design/letter-height constraints;
4. named project process and responsible parties;
5. verified electrical/outdoor technical package;
6. itemised inclusions, exclusions and price treatment;
7. real service area, warranty and support path.

### Prohibited launch messages

- `passt an jede Markise`;
- `genehmigungsfrei` or `garantiert genehmigungsfähig`;
- `wasserdicht`, an IP class or wind class without complete-system evidence;
- `mehr Gäste`, `mehr Umsatz`, `höhere Frequenz`, ROI or payback claims;
- `Made in Germany`;
- `deutschlandweite Montage`;
- price-from, delivery-time, warranty or free-consultation claims not marked `Approved`;
- supplier/customer logos or project photographs without permission.

## Channel plan

### Priority and role

| Priority | Channel | Role in the pilot | Why now | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Local account-based outreach | Create demand and learn real façades/objections | Category search is narrow; suitable sites can be visually identified | Labour intensive; imagery and contact use must be lawful |
| 1 | Awning/sign/electrical partners | Reach trigger moments and deliver field capability | Partners see replacement/rebrand work and installed systems | Responsibility and margin conflict |
| 2 | One organic product pillar | Explain the category and support every sales conversation | Public result sample has few strong exact pages | Needs real proof and technical inputs |
| 2 | Exact/phrase Google Search | Capture existing exact category/retrofit intent | High product fit, but volume unknown | Small volume and severe generic-LED noise |
| 3 | Case-led hospitality outreach/PR | Build category credibility after paid projects | Visual transformation can travel through local trade networks | Becomes empty promotion without real case |
| Deferred | PMax, broad match, display, remarketing | Scale later | Requires conversion history, assets and consent | Waste, noise and weak message control |

### Local account outreach experiment

1. Define a compliant local list of visibly suitable restaurants/cafés; do not scrape or store
   unnecessary personal data.
2. Code only business-level facts needed for the test: frontage, awning, evening hours, visible
   branding, trigger signal and contact route.
3. For a small subset, create a restrained private concept visualisation. Do not publish or imply a
   client relationship.
4. Offer the compatibility check, not a generic sales call.
5. Record disposition: incompatible, no need, timing, price, landlord/permission, current sign,
   interested review, quote, order.

Proposed 30-day learning sample: 30–50 target accounts, with results reported as counts rather than
percentages until the denominator is meaningful.

Stop or revise the message if fewer than 5 operators agree to a substantive compatibility/need
conversation after 30 qualified contacts. This is a proposed learning rule, not a market benchmark.

### Partner experiment

Interview at least:

- 3 awning installers;
- 2 advertising/sign companies;
- 2 electrical contractors;
- optionally 2 architects/façade planners with hospitality work.

Test:

- frequency of replaceable-valance requests;
- common awning/Keder geometries and failure cases;
- willingness to measure/install a supplier product;
- required margin/referral structure;
- electrical and permit ownership;
- service and rework expectations;
- whether the partner wants a supply SKU, managed referral or white-label relationship.

Do not select a programme model before those conversations.

## Landing brief

### Page decision

Create one canonical product pillar after owner acceptance:

Proposed route: `/beleuchteter-markisen-volant`

Whether it becomes the homepage or a product route is an architecture/content decision at scaffold
time. Do not simultaneously target the same intent with a near-duplicate homepage and route.

Do not launch separate:

- gastronomy, hotel or retail pages without segment-specific projects and copy;
- city pages without verified local service and unique local value;
- a `nachrüsten` duplicate that repeats the pillar;
- empty references, pricing or partner pages.

### Page job

Within the first viewport and first explanatory section, a visitor must understand:

1. this replaces only the valance on an existing commercial awning;
2. it displays a custom illuminated logo or wordmark;
3. compatibility is checked before a price or production promise;
4. the next action is to submit the project for review.

### Information architecture

| Order | Section | User question | Required evidence/content |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hero | What exactly is this and is it for my business? | Exact category, existing-awning wording, primary CTA, real/sample image |
| 2 | Day/night transformation | Why illumination instead of ordinary print? | Same object by day and night; no traffic/ROI claim |
| 3 | Compatibility / retrofit | Am I buying a new awning, and will it fit? | One labelled sequence: existing awning → compatibility review → new illuminated valance; what is checked, what changes, what remains and three initial inputs |
| 4 | Flagship and variants | What can be made? | Outcome-led standard plus verified panel/letter limits, not lifestyle model inflation |
| 5 | Process | Who does what? | Check → measure → design proof → quote → production → installation/electrical → handover |
| 6 | Scope and cost drivers | What will I pay for? | Product, design, freight, field work, electrical, access, local documentation; VAT treatment |
| 7 | Technical and local-rule boundary | Is it safe/allowed? | Verified ratings/docs, qualified connection and object-specific permission note |
| 8 | Real project evidence | Has this worked in a comparable venue? | Rights-cleared day/night case with dimensions, actual scope and no invented outcome |
| 9 | Alternatives | Why not print, add a lightbox or replace the awning? | Honest comparison and cases where LICHTSAUM is not suitable |
| 10 | FAQ | What still blocks my decision? | Compatibility, power, permission, timing, warranty, maintenance—only approved answers |
| 11 | Project-check form | What do you need from me? | Minimal qualified fields, clear privacy explanation and success/error states |

The visual implementation follows `../../DESIGN.md`. It should use real or rights-cleared sample
material, not supplier/client imagery copied from competitor pages.

### Hero direction

Proposed:

> **Aus Ihrer bestehenden Markise wird Markenlicht.**  
> Ein individuell gestalteter, beleuchteter Markisen-Volant für bestehende Gewerbemarkisen. Vor
> Fertigung prüfen wir Markise, Einbausituation und Stromweg.  
> **Projekt prüfen lassen**

If measurement, installation or electrical coordination is owner-confirmed, mention it in a
separate factual scope line. Do not compress unverified “full service” into the hero.

### Compatibility input

The first review should request only what is needed:

- business/venue name and postcode;
- contact person and a private contact channel;
- front, side and valance/attachment close-up photos;
- overall awning width and current valance height if known;
- logo/text file or plain-text requirement;
- power nearby: yes / no / unknown;
- desired scope: supply / measurement / removal / installation / electrical / unknown;
- tenant/owner and permission status: known / unknown;
- short project timing context.

Do not put contact details, exact address, free text or filenames into analytics, URLs or page
titles. File uploads require type/size checks, malware-safe handling, access control, retention and
metadata/privacy review. The form must work without marketing consent.

### Form and conversion

- The CTA begins a project review, not a guaranteed quote.
- Server validation and antispam/rate limiting are mandatory.
- A valid submission produces exactly one non-personal `lead_id`.
- The only primary bidding conversion remains server-confirmed lead acceptance, as defined in
  [Measurement Plan](../marketing/measurement-plan.md).
- Compatibility rejection, quote issuance, paid order and installation completion should be kept
  in the CRM/operations funnel. Any analytics events must first be added to the typed measurement
  contract and must not contain PII.

### Pricing presentation

First release:

- explain what changes price;
- state whether displayed/project prices are net plus VAT or gross, based on the approved audience
  and legal presentation;
- provide a project-specific preliminary range after review if operations can honour it;
- list exclusions clearly;
- do not expose the supplier catalogue as a customer tariff.

Later estimator:

1. validate at least 10 complete estimates and 3 paid installed projects;
2. prove standard-case quoted variance can remain within ±15%;
3. expose the preliminary range **before** any contact gate;
4. let contact details save the project or request the confirmed quote, not unlock the number;
5. separate fabricated product, design, delivery, removal/installation, electrical and special
   access/local documentation;
6. show net/gross and VAT treatment;
7. route every incompatibility/unknown to manual review;
8. never imply permit, installation date or compatibility approval.

The public PixelRing calculator shows that an ungated preliminary price already exists as a
competitive pattern. One example does not make it a category norm, and it does not prove that
LICHTSAUM currently has stable cost rules.

### Calculator auto-eligibility, later

Potential standard-case inputs:

- valance overall width and height;
- panel format and 1–3 illuminated zones;
- logo/text layout tier;
- cable sleeve;
- colour filter;
- control option;
- delivery postcode band;
- measurement/removal/installation/electrical selections.

Manual-only:

- inadequate photos/measurements;
- non-removable, curved or damaged awning;
- unresolved movement/cable path;
- high access or traffic management;
- heritage/local-rule uncertainty;
- unknown power or outdoor driver location;
- landlord authority unresolved;
- more than three zones, unusual controls or multi-site work.

### Proof pack required before public release

- owner-approved company and contact identity;
- one inspected physical sample;
- supplier technical documentation and approved installation method;
- real service geography and responsible contractors;
- one rights-cleared day/night case, or a clearly labelled owned sample if no client case exists;
- approved product, process, price, warranty and timing claims;
- compatibility checklist and rejected-case list;
- legal/privacy text and file-upload data flow;
- real error, confirmation and follow-up process.

## 30/60/90 execution

### Days 0–30

**Product**

- contract the supplier/commercial assumptions;
- inspect technical declarations and build one flagship sample;
- define compatibility, measurement and installation checklists;
- name the party responsible for electrical, authority and warranty work.

**Market**

- complete 10 primary-customer interviews;
- complete at least 7 partner interviews across awning/sign/electrical roles;
- manually code 300–500 façades if resources permit; at minimum code the first 100 and report
  sampling bias.

**Commercial**

- build the itemised quote template and direct-cost sheet;
- define the budget qualification and minimum complete order;
- approach 30–50 qualified local accounts.

**Content**

- create a private low-fidelity page prototype and sales one-pager;
- photograph the owned sample by day and night;
- do not publish or scaffold the production site.

### Days 31–60

- conduct at least 10 compatibility reviews and 5 formal quotes;
- seek 2 paid pilots;
- record labour, supplier, freight, access and electrical cost separately;
- test direct outreach and at least 2 partner relationships;
- decide whether the flagship format fits enough real cases;
- update the claims register only with reviewed owner/supplier evidence;
- prepare the canonical landing copy with real scope and case material.

### Days 61–90

- target 3 completed paid projects and 10 total formal quotes;
- calculate actual direct margin, quote-to-order conversion and rework;
- obtain one partner-sourced qualified opportunity;
- document every rejection reason and non-standard cost;
- decide `GO`, `REVISE` or `NO-GO` against the pre-set gates;
- only after `GO`, approve production landing implementation and a small exact/phrase Search test.

## Scorecard

| Layer | Metric | Why it matters | Initial decision use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Installed-base fit | Suitable façades / audited façades | Replaces the largest TAM assumption | Segment/geography viability |
| Technical fit | Accepted / reviewed projects | Tests product compatibility | Standard vs manual share |
| Demand | Substantive buyer conversations, reviews, quotes, paid orders | Distinguishes interest from purchase | Product-market evidence |
| Price | Rejection reason and accepted net price | Tests willingness to pay | Offer/pricing |
| Delivery | Direct hours/cost, repeat visit, failure/rework | Tests operational repeatability | Margin and warranty reserve |
| Funnel | Review → quote → order | Sets real CPL/CAC ceiling | Channel budget |
| Channel | Qualified opportunities and wins by source | Shows whether partners/search/direct work | GTM allocation |
| Evidence | Usable day/night cases and rights | Enables premium landing and ads | Content readiness |

Raw traffic, social engagement, form starts and button clicks are diagnostic only. They cannot
substitute for a paid project or a server-confirmed valid lead.
