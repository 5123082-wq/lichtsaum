# Market and Competitor Evidence

Status: `Verified`  
Scope: dated source ledger and derived calculations  
Model status: `Proposed`; every non-source input is labelled  
Last reviewed: 2026-07-30  
Geography: Germany, with Berlin/Brandenburg pilot detail and French supply benchmarks  
Legal note: this is product and engineering research, not legal advice

## Evidence conclusion

Five conclusions are supportable:

1. **The physical category exists.** Multiple German and French companies sell illuminated textile
   valances or closely related professional awning products. A current trade catalogue provides
   standard panel sizes, components and net supplier prices.
2. **Germany is an early, fragmented category.** Exact retrofit pages exist, but terminology,
   public pricing, installation scope and technical disclosure are inconsistent.
3. **France has a more developed supply layer.** Manufacturers, webshop configuration and a
   full-service installer model are visible. This proves supply feasibility, not German demand.
4. **The official business universe is large enough for a pilot but is not an installed-base
   count.** No official source was found for street-facing awnings, replaceable valances or purchase
   frequency.
5. **Regulation, electrical work and compatibility are part of the product.** A textile replacement
   can still be an illuminated advertising system, affect a protected façade and require a safe
   building connection. `Genehmigungsfrei` (procedure-free) must never be used as a universal
   promise.

The evidence supports `VALIDATE FIRST`, not a national `GO`.

## Scope and evidence rules

Included:

- B2B retrofit of an existing commercial awning;
- new textile valance, illuminated logo/text field, driver, cabling and controls;
- restaurants, cafés, selected bars, hotels, shops and professional partners;
- product supply, measurement, installation, electrical and authority boundaries;
- exact German category/search evidence and close substitutes.

Excluded:

- new residential or commercial awning market sizing;
- B2C outdoor living and ambient terrace lighting;
- general signs/lightboxes unless used as a substitute;
- estimates derived from generic awning-market reports;
- traffic, backlink, revenue or client-result estimates without auditable data.

Source types are kept distinct:

- **Official:** government/statistical/regulatory source; high confidence for what it directly says.
- **Direct company:** a company’s own catalogue, page or calculator; high confidence that the offer
  was published, but only medium confidence in performance, client and superlative claims.
- **Observed sample:** dated public result composition; evidence of discoverability and ambiguity,
  not search volume or ranking stability.
- **Assumption:** analyst input used only in the model; must be replaced by primary validation.

### Business-change triggers

Destatis business-demography data for 2024 reports:

- retail WZ47: 23,943 births and 36,441 preliminary closures;
- accommodation WZ55: 3,641 births and 4,883 preliminary closures;
- gastronomy WZ56: 18,815 births and 20,867 preliminary closures.

This verifies a meaningful annual pool of new or changing operators. It does **not** verify that an
opening, closure, rebrand or textile replacement produces demand for an illuminated valance
(E-009).

Only 36% of gastronomy businesses founded in 2018 were still active five years later. That fact
supports careful credit terms, modular service and price sensitivity; it is not evidence of product
demand (E-010).

### Missing installed-base evidence

No official or reliable German source was found for:

- customer-facing façades with an awning;
- the share with a removable textile valance;
- the share technically able to carry the illuminated assembly;
- the share trading after dusk;
- the frequency of valance replacement, rebranding or logo change;
- the share choosing illumination rather than unlit print;
- the share of independent operators in the selected counts.

Commercial fabric sellers say valances weather faster than the protected main fabric and are often
replaced separately, but no universal replacement interval is supportable. Replacement cadence is
therefore an assumption, not a claim.

The best next evidence is a stratified audit of 300–500 real façades plus interviews with operators,
awning installers, sign makers and electrical contractors. The audit should code frontage,
existing awning, detachable valance, opening after dusk, advertising/heritage constraints,
existing illuminated sign and independent/chain ownership.

## TAM/SAM/SOM model

### Method

This is an annual revenue opportunity model:

`segment enterprises × suitable-site share × annual trigger × illuminated-choice share × net project value`

The model does not silently convert all businesses into buyers. It separates:

1. the verified target-enterprise denominator;
2. estimated physical/commercial suitability;
3. a replacement/opening/rebranding trigger;
4. selection of the illuminated option;
5. the internal net project-price scenario.

### Assumptions

| Input | Low | Base | High | Status / interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Suitable selected-gastronomy share | 8% | 18% | 30% | `Proposed` model assumption: frontage + compatible awning + evening relevance + rules |
| Suitable hotel share | 3% | 8% | 15% | `Proposed` model assumption |
| Suitable stationary-retail share | 1% | 3% | 8% | `Proposed` model assumption; heterogeneous and often adequately served by unlit print |
| Annual trigger among suitable sites | 10% | 15% | 20% | `Proposed` model assumption: opening, rebrand, refurbishment, damage/replacement |
| Illuminated choice among triggered sites | 5% | 10% | 15% | `Proposed` model assumption: category adoption at an installed project price |
| Combined annual activation | 0.5% | 1.5% | 3.0% | Calculated |
| Average net project value | EUR 2,500 | EUR 5,200 | EUR 8,000 | `Proposed` model assumption; not public pricing |

The annual trigger is consistent with a market containing regular openings and closures, but the
product-choice rate is entirely unverified. The two inputs must be tested separately in interviews
and paid quotes.

### First-year SOM envelope

| Scenario | Paid projects | Average net project | Net revenue |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | 2 | EUR 2,500 | EUR 5,000 |
| Base | 8 | EUR 5,200 | EUR 41,600 |
| High | 18 | EUR 8,000 | EUR 144,000 |

This is a capacity and validation envelope, not an evidence-backed forecast. It is intentionally
bounded below SAM. Recalculate it as:

`SOM = min(confirmed annual delivery capacity, annual SAM, qualified opportunities × close rate)`.

Inputs still required are crews, installations per week, quote-to-order conversion, approval time,
repeat visits, warranty reserve and partner capacity.

### Interpretation

- The category can justify an economical local pilot.
- The base case is more credible as a specialist product line and partner-enabled service than as a
  standalone nationwide direct-sales organisation.
- Search demand alone is unlikely to produce the modelled base volume; account outreach and
  installed-base partners are necessary.
- The model should be replaced, not merely refined, after the façade audit and first 10–15 real
  project reviews.

## Regulation and engineering evidence

### Advertising systems and protected façades

| Geography | Verified rule/source statement | Product implication | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Berlin | Visible stationary illuminated advertising can be a `Werbeanlage`. BauO Bln contains procedure-free size/context exceptions, but other public-law and heritage rules still apply. `Genehmigungsfreiheit` means freedom from a particular construction procedure, not guaranteed legal
permissibility. The final determination is object- and municipality-specific.

### Electrical and product responsibility

- NAV §13 generally reserves work on the building electrical installation behind the service
  connection to the network operator or a registered installation company. A fixed building
  connection should therefore have an appropriately registered contractor and documented scope
  (E-030).
- A plug-in SELV configuration may have a different installation boundary, but that cannot be
  assumed until the complete supplied system and instructions are known.
- German product-safety and electromagnetic-compatibility duties apply to the responsible economic
  operator for the actual assembly placed on the market. CE marking is not a general quality badge
  and does not replace safe project installation (E-031).
- If LICHTSAUM combines or materially modifies panels, driver, cabling, fastening and intended use,
  its manufacturer/integrator role must be determined before a paid pilot.
- Commercial operation can create inspection duties after installation/change and during use;
  exact scope depends on the equipment and the operator’s risk assessment (E-032).

Still unverified: complete-system ingress protection, operating temperature, UV/weather exposure,
fire behaviour, strain relief, movement fatigue, added wind/load behaviour, maintenance interval,
spares, repair method and end-of-life responsibility. These are engineering gates, not copy topics.
